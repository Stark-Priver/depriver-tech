Six things students in Tanzania tell each other about tech. Some are lies, some are half true. Here's the honest version, and what to do instead. *Hadithi sita unazosikia kila siku.*

## 1. “Lazima uwe genius wa hesabu.” Half true

Most web and app work is logic, patience and searching for answers. School maths is enough to start. Data science, AI and games do need more maths, but later, when you choose those paths.

**Do this instead:** start with web or apps, and learn more maths when a path needs it.

## 2. “Bila MacBook huwezi ku-code.” False

Any laptop with 8 GB RAM and an SSD is enough. Linux makes old laptops fast again. A Mac is only required if you publish iOS apps.

**Do this instead:** use the laptop you have. Try Linux or WSL to make it lighter.

## 3. “Umechelewa, una miaka 25.” False

People start tech at 25, 35, even 45. Employers check what you can build, not your age, and your other experience is an advantage.

**Do this instead:** pick one free course this week and finish it.

## 4. “Degree haina maana kwenye tech.” Half true

A degree opens doors: HR filters, government jobs and visas often ask for one. But skills and projects are what get you hired. The best plan is both.

**Do this instead:** stay in school, and push one project to GitHub every month.

## 5. “AI itachukua kazi zote za tech.” False

AI writes code fast, and also makes mistakes. Someone must understand, check and own the result. People who use AI well will replace people who don't.

**Do this instead:** learn the basics yourself, then ask AI to explain, not to do it for you.

## 6. “Cybersecurity ni ku-hack watu.” False

Security work is mostly defence: protecting systems and people. Breaking into accounts without permission is a crime under Tanzania's Cybercrimes Act, 2015. Practise only on legal labs.

**Do this instead:** start a free beginner path on TryHackMe.

“Nisaidie code” gets you generic code you can't use. A good prompt gets code that fits your project and teaches you. *Uliza kwa ufasaha.* (Pairs with our earlier post on using AI as a tutor, not a copy machine.)

## Vague in, vague out

Ask “make me a login system” and you may get Node.js, MongoDB, JWT and React, when you use PHP and MySQL. AI fills every gap you leave with a guess.

## The formula: C · G · C · F

1. **Context:** who you are, your stack, what you tried.
2. **Goal:** what you want, in one sentence.
3. **Constraints:** your version, no new libraries, beginner level.
4. **Format:** steps, a short example, a hint, a table…

## A strong prompt

> I'm a 2nd-year student. PHP 8 + MySQL, no framework. I need a login page for my FYP. Explain the steps first, then show only the login code. Use `password_verify()`. Keep it under 40 lines.

## Prompts that teach you

- “Explain this code line by line, like I'm in first year.”
- “Give me 3 small exercises on loops, without answers.”
- “Here's my code. Don't fix it: give me one hint.”
- “Quiz me with 5 questions on SQL JOINs.”

Use AI to practise, so you can do it alone in the exam room.

## Debug with AI properly

Paste the **full** error, the **smallest** code that fails, what you expected, what happened and what you tried, then ask for the **cause** first, before the fix. Writing this out often solves the bug before you send it.

## Stay in charge

Never paste secrets (passwords, API keys, client data, IDs), run and test everything, understand it before you submit it, and follow your course's AI rules. AI is your assistant; you are the developer.

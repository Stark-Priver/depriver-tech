My first field practical training was at MUST in 2024, and it shaped me. On 7 October 2026 I went back, this time to stand in front of students finishing their own FPT and talk about **Life in Uni vs Real World in Tech**: what uni teaches, and what the real world in tech expects. *Nimekaa kiti chako*: I sat in your seat.

## Part 1: The six rounds

| Life in Uni | Real world in tech | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Code on paper | Code on screen | Type your paper code on a computer the same day. |
| Memorise it | Find it | Learn to read official documentation. |
| Group work | Teamwork | Do your part, and keep it on GitHub. |
| Toy projects | Real users | Build one thing a real person uses, every semester. |
| Diagrams | Production | Put your projects online, even on a free tier. |
| Grades | GitHub | Keep three projects you can demo in two minutes. |

Learn it in class. Prove it in projects. *Shule inakupa msingi, nyumba unajenga wewe.* The longer version is in [Darasani vs Kazini](/blog/darasani-vs-kazini/).

## Part 2: Start now, finish strong

Six habits to start before you graduate: type up your paper code the same day, ship a real project every semester, live on GitHub, use AI as a tutor and not a copy machine, treat every project like real work, and respect the fundamentals.

Before FPT ends: write a logbook that shows skill, ask for a recommendation letter, keep your contacts and collect proof. The full week-by-week guide is in [Make your FPT count](/blog/make-your-fpt-count/).

## Part 3: Money talk

- **Use your student status.** Free pro tools, cloud credits and certificates, and they expire when you graduate. The full list is in [Free tools for students](/blog/free-tools-for-students/).
- **A gig pays you twice:** money and experience. Start local with websites, IT support and design.
- **Betting and forex are not side hustles.** And if someone asks you to pay to get a job, it's a scam.

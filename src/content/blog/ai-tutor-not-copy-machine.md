AI can make you learn twice as fast, or it can quietly stop you learning at all. The difference is how you ask. Use it like a copy machine and it works until the exam, until the interview ("walk me through your code"), until the 2 a.m. bug that no chat can see. Use it like a tutor and you get faster *and* smarter. If you can't explain it, you didn't learn it. *Akili ni yako.*

## Five rounds: copy machine vs tutor

**1. Ask why, not just what.**
- Copy machine: *"Write my assignment on linked lists."*
- Tutor: *"Explain linked lists like I'm new to them, with a real-life example. Then give me one small exercise, but don't show the answer."*

**2. Understand the error.**
- Copy machine: *"Fix my code."* (pastes 200 lines)
- Tutor: *"Here's my error and the function it points to. What does this error mean, and where should I look first? Give me hints, not the fix."*

**3. Let it test you.**
- Copy machine: *"Summarise chapter 5 for my exam."*
- Tutor: *"Quiz me with 5 questions on SQL joins, one at a time. Wait for my answer, then tell me what I got wrong and why."*

**4. Write it first, then compare.**
- Copy machine: *"Write a login page in PHP for me."*
- Tutor: *"I wrote this login function myself. What would a senior developer improve, especially security, and why?"*

**5. Check everything.** AI can be confidently wrong.
- Copy machine: *"AI said it, so it must be right."*
- Tutor: *"Show me where this is in the official docs, and give me a small test so I can check it works on my machine."*

## Five house rules

1. **Know your school's rules.** Ask your lecturers what's allowed in assignments.
2. **Never paste secrets:** no passwords, client data or office code.
3. **Try first, ask second.** Twenty minutes stuck, then ask for a hint.
4. **Explain it back.** If you can't teach it to a friend, go again.
5. **Credit it honestly.** Say when AI helped. Integrity is a skill too.

AI is a tool in your hand, not a brain in your place.

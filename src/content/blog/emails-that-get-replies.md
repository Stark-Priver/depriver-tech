From `cutieboy99@gmail.com`, subject “hi”: “habari naomba kazi yoyote nina cv”. It will be ignored. *Barua pepe inayojibiwa.*

## Why it gets ignored

An unprofessional address, an empty subject, no name, role or details, “kazi yoyote” (which sounds desperate), and a CV mentioned but not attached. Recruiters decide in about ten seconds, so make it easy.

## Six parts of a good email

1. **A clear subject:** “Internship application: Backend (Asha Juma)”.
2. **A greeting with a name:** “Dear Ms Mushi,” if you know it.
3. **Who you are, in one line.**
4. **What you want:** one specific request.
5. **Why you, or the details:** two or three facts and a link.
6. **Thanks and a signature:** name, phone, LinkedIn or GitHub.

If it doesn't fit on a phone screen, it's too long.

## To a company

> **Subject:** Internship application: Backend developer (Asha Juma)
>
> Dear Ms Mushi,
> I'm a third-year Computer Science student at MUST, applying for your backend internship from June to August.
> I've built a shop stock API in Laravel (link below) and I'm comfortable with SQL and Git.
> My CV is attached. Thank you for your time.
> Asha Juma · 07XX XXX XXX · github.com/ashajuma

Name the file `Firstname_Lastname_CV.pdf`, never `cv_final2.pdf`.

## To a lecturer

> **Subject:** CS 2201: question about Assignment 2, part B
>
> Dear Dr Mwakyusa,
> I'm Asha Juma, CS 2201, Group B. In Assignment 2 part B, should the API return all orders, or only today's? I've read the brief and the week 5 slides.
> Thank you. Asha Juma, Reg. No. …

Show you tried first, and ask one clear question.

## To a client

> **Subject:** Quotation: Duka la Mama Neema website
>
> Dear Mama Neema,
> Thank you for meeting me on Tuesday. As promised, the quotation is attached: 5 pages, mobile friendly, ready in 3 weeks. With a 50% deposit I can start on Monday.

Always end with a clear next step.

## Small habits

Use a professional address (firstname.lastname@gmail.com is free), read it out loud before sending, follow up once after 5 to 7 working days, reply within a day (even “nimepokea, nitajibu kesho”), and stay formal until they relax the tone.

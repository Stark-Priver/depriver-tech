A client asks “bei gani?” and you say “toa chochote”. Weeks later you're still adding features for TZS 50,000. Here's a better way. *Usijiuze bure.* All numbers below are **examples**, not market rates.

## The method: hours × rate + costs + buffer

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| Your hours | 30 hours |
| × your hourly rate | × TZS 12,000 |
| = your time | 360,000 |
| + costs (data, transport) | 30,000 |
| + buffer 15% (surprises) | 60,000 |
| **= quote** | **≈ 450,000** |

Your rate depends on your skill, the client and the city. Ask seniors what they charge, start a bit lower, and raise it with every project.

## Costs students forget

- **Domain and hosting.** Better: the client pays and owns them directly.
- **Meetings and travel.** Three bajaji trips add up.
- **Data bundles** for uploads, calls and research.
- **Paid themes, plugins and SMS credits.** List them or ask the client to buy them.
- **Support after launch.** Agree on a period, e.g. two weeks of free fixes.

If it costs you money, it goes in the quote.

## Write the scope

**Included:** 5 pages, a contact form, mobile layout, 2 rounds of changes.
**Not included:** online payments, a mobile app, writing all the text, monthly updates.

New request? “Sawa, hiyo ni kazi mpya. Nitakutumia nukuu yake.”

## Getting paid

1. **50% deposit to start.** No deposit, no start.
2. **Show progress** with a link they can click every week.
3. **50% before handover**, before final files, logins or going live.
4. **A receipt for every payment**, with the mobile money reference.

For bigger jobs, split it into milestones (design, build, launch) and get paid at each.

## Send a real quote

A written quotation (*nukuu ya bei*) with the items, the total, the deposit and how long it's valid (e.g. 14 days) looks professional and protects you both.

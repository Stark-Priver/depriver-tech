Late at night your phone shows “No service”. Minutes later your email password changes and your mobile money is gone. That's a SIM swap. *Usipuuze “No service”.*

## What it is

Criminals don't steal your phone, they steal your **number**: they get it moved to a SIM card they hold, and your SIM goes dead. Your number receives the OTP codes for mobile money, banks, email and WhatsApp, so whoever holds it can reset them. Your number is a key to your accounts.

## How it usually goes

1. They collect your details: leaked IDs, fake calls, your social media.
2. They request a new SIM, pretending to be you or with help from an insider.
3. Your SIM goes dead, often late at night.
4. They reset your accounts, because the OTPs now arrive on their phone.
5. Money moves fast: mobile money, bank, gone.

## Warning signs

- “No service” for a long time in a place where your network is usually fine.
- An SMS about a SIM change you didn't request.
- Calls and SMS stop arriving; friends say your number rings somewhere else.
- Login or password alerts from your email or apps.

## The first 10 minutes

1. **Borrow a phone and call your network.** Ask them to block the number and the new SIM.
2. **Call your bank** to freeze cards and online banking.
3. **Change your email password** from a computer, because email resets everything else.
4. **Warn family on WhatsApp.** Scammers will message them as you.
5. **Report it** to the police cybercrime desk, with dates and references.

Write your network's and bank's helpline numbers on paper today.

## Make yourself a hard target

Never post your ID or full details (no NIDA photos, no birth date with your phone number), use a PIN that isn't your birthday, use an authenticator app instead of SMS for email and social media where possible, and never share OTPs. No real staff member will ever ask for a code sent to you.

## For developers

Don't trust SMS OTP alone for big actions; add a PIN, app approval or a delay. Some networks and gateways offer a SIM-swap check, so ask yours. Step up verification on new devices, and alert users when their phone number or password changes. Security that only uses SMS is only as strong as the SIM.

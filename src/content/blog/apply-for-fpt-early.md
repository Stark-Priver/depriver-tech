The best FPT places fill early. Start this month. *Omba mapema.* (Then read [Make your FPT count](/blog/make-your-fpt-count/) for what to do once you're in.)

## Why early

Popular companies take a limited number of students, some have deadlines (miss one and you wait a year), supervisors choose people who asked early and clearly, and a “no” in March still leaves time for plan B. A great FPT can become your first job.

## Documents

- CV (one page, PDF)
- Cover letter or application email
- University introduction letter
- Latest results (transcript)
- GitHub or portfolio link
- Copy of your student ID

Scan everything into one folder on your phone and email.

## Where tech students go

Banks and fintech · telecoms · software houses · government agencies · hospitals and NGOs · startups. Also ask your department's placement office and last year's students.

## How to approach

1. **Email a clear application:** subject, who you are, dates, CV attached. (Our [emails that get replies](/blog/emails-that-get-replies/) post has a template.)
2. **Visit with printed documents** and ask for HR or the IT manager, politely.
3. **Follow up after a week:** short, friendly, once.
4. **Get the acceptance in writing**, because your university will need the letter.

## Month by month

| Month | Do this |
|---|---|
| March | List 10 places, prepare documents |
| April | Send all applications |
| May | Follow up, visit, plan B |
| June | Confirm, get the letter |
| FPT | Learn, build, keep a logbook |

Your university's dates may differ, so check with your coordinator.

## Plan B

Ask lecturers and alumni (many placements come through people), try smaller companies, offer a clear skill (“I can help with your website or IT support”), and build something real for a local business anyway. Ten applications with no answer is normal. Keep going.

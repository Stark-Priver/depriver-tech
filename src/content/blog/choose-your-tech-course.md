Results are out, and everyone around you has an opinion about what you should study. This post is for anyone finishing Form Four or Form Six who wants a career in tech. Here's how to choose properly. Chagua njia yako.

## Step 1: Know your road

- **After Form Four:** an Ordinary Diploma (NTA levels 4 to 6) at a college. Check the courses and colleges on [NACTVET](https://www.nactvet.go.tz). Finish with a good GPA and you can join a degree later through equivalent entry, or start working.
- **After Form Six:** a bachelor's degree, usually 3 years (4 for engineering programmes). Your A-Level subject combination decides which programmes you can join.

A diploma is not a failure, and a degree is not a guarantee. *Njia zote zinafika.*

## Step 2: Pick a course that fits you

| Course | What you'll learn | Pick it if |
|---|---|---|
| Computer Science | How software works: algorithms, data, programming | you love solving puzzles |
| Software Engineering | Building, testing and shipping real apps in teams | you want to build products |
| Information Technology | Systems, databases and networks that run organisations | you like making tech work for people |
| Data Science & AI | Statistics, data analysis and machine learning | you're strong in maths |
| Cyber Security | Protecting systems, networks and data from attacks | you think like a detective |
| Computer Engineering | Hardware meets software: circuits and embedded systems | you open up gadgets to see inside |
| Networks & Telecom | How data travels: networks, mobile and the internet | you want to keep the country online |
| Business IT / Information Systems | Systems that help companies run | you like both tech and business |

Don't judge a course by its name: read the modules. Not sure yet? Computer Science keeps the most doors open.

## Step 4: Check the entry rules

You need two principal passes at A-Level, but *which* subjects depends on the programme. Some real examples from the 2026/27 guidebook:

- **UDSM, Computer Science:** Advanced Mathematics and Physics or Computer Science
- **UDOM, Software Engineering:** Mathematics and either Physics or Computer Science
- **MUST, Computer Science:** Advanced Mathematics and Physics or Chemistry, plus at least a D in Basic Mathematics and English at O-Level
- **ARU, Data Science & AI:** Mathematics and one of Physics, Geography, Chemistry, Computer Science, Economics, Business Studies or Accountancy

Maths opens most doors. *Hesabu ni ufunguo.* Download the guidebook and match it against your own combination.

## Step 5: Research before you apply

- Is it accredited? Check the TCU or NACTVET lists.
- Read the curriculum, not just the course name.
- Are there enough labs and computers for every student?
- Does it have field practical and links with real companies?
- Where do its alumni work? Look them up on LinkedIn.
- What's the real total cost: fees, HESLB loan eligibility, rent?
- Talk to current students. They'll tell you the truth.

You're choosing three years of your life. Spend at least three days researching. *Chunguza kabla ya kuchagua.*

## Step 6: Make your own choice

I got Division I in Form Four, and everyone expected me to go to A-Level. I chose a Computer Science diploma at MUST instead. Teachers, parents and relatives said it was the wrong choice, and some people called it a college "for failed students". I was hired as a software engineer before I even graduated.

Don't choose for prestige, or because your friends did. Choose the path where you'll actually learn. *Uamuzi ni wako, si wa watu.*

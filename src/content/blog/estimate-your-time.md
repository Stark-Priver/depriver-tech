“Itachukua siku 2.” Two weeks later… We've all been there. *Kadirio la kweli.*

## Why estimates slip

You only count the coding (not testing, fixing, meetings or deploying), unknowns hide inside (a new library, a strange bug, missing data), interruptions happen (classes, family, power cuts, no bundle), and we imagine the perfect day where everything works first time. Even seniors get it wrong; they just plan for it.

## Break it down

| Feature: login | Hours |
|---|---|
| Login form (screen) | 3 |
| API endpoint + validation | 4 |
| Password hashing + sessions | 3 |
| Error messages | 2 |
| **Total** | **12** |

If a task is bigger than one day, split it again.

## Add the hidden work

Building might be three days. Then add testing, fixing bugs, review, deploying and docs, and the real job is closer to nine.

## Give a range

| Situation | Multiply by |
|---|---|
| Done it before | × 1.3 |
| Similar, some new parts | × 1.5 |
| Brand new tech for you | × 2 |
| Depends on other people | × 2 or more |

Say “3 to 5 days”, not “3 days”. Under-promise and over-deliver, never the opposite.

## Track your own ratio

Keep a simple sheet: task, guess, real. After a few weeks you'll know your own ratio (often about × 2) and can use it for every new estimate.

## Late? Say it early

> “Habari boss, login itachelewa: password reset ilikuwa ngumu kuliko nilivyofikiri. Nitamaliza Alhamisi badala ya leo. Nimeshamaliza fomu na API.”

Say what's late and why (one line), the new date, and what's already done. Never surprise people on the deadline day.

Your first tech interview doesn't have to be scary. The same few questions come up almost every time, and you can prepare for all of them. *Usiende bila maandalizi.*

## Win it before you arrive

1. **Research the company:** what they build, who they serve, recent news.
2. **Test your demo:** open your project and run it the night before.
3. **Re-read your CV.** They'll ask about every line on it.
4. **Plan the trip.** Arrive 15 minutes early. If it's online, charge your laptop, buy data and find a quiet room.
5. **Dress one step up.** Neat and simple, and smart casual is safe.

## Q1: "Tell me about yourself."

**Present → past → future, in 60 seconds.**

> *"I'm a Computer Science graduate who builds web apps. During FPT I set up an office network and built an equipment tracker. Now I want to grow as a backend developer here."*

Practise it out loud until it sounds natural, not memorised.

## Q2: "Walk me through a project you built."

**Problem → your part → tools → result → what you'd improve.** Pick the project you know best, show it running if you can, and be honest about what was hard. They're testing whether you understand your own code.

## Q3: "Why should we hire you?"

**Their need + your proof + your attitude.**

> *"You need someone who can support your systems. I did that daily during FPT, I learn fast, and I finish what I start."*

Read the job advert and answer in their words.

## Q4: A question you can't answer

Say *"sijui"* well: be honest, then explain how you'd find out.

> *"I haven't used Kubernetes yet. I'd start with the official docs, try it on a small project, and ask a senior. Here's how I learnt Docker last month…"*

Guessing loses trust. Being honest and curious wins it. *Sijui si kosa.*

## Q5: "Do you have any questions for us?"

Always say yes. Ask about the work and how you'd grow:

- "What would my first 90 days look like?"
- "How does the team review code?"
- "Is there mentorship for juniors?"

Never say "no questions". It sounds like you don't care.

## After: follow up within 24 hours

Send a short thank-you email or message: thank them, mention one thing you enjoyed learning about the team, and say you're excited about the role. Then wait patiently. If you hear nothing after a week or two, one short follow-up is fine. Didn't get it? Ask for feedback, because every interview is practice. *Hujashindwa, umejifunza.*

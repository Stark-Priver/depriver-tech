Hackathon and DevFest season is here. If you've never joined one, this is your sign. *Usiogope, jaribu.*

## What it is

Teams of 2 to 5 build a working prototype in 24 to 48 hours, then present it to judges. Prizes are nice; the real win is what you learn and who you meet. Beginners are welcome, and organisers want you there.

**Where to find them:** Google DevFests and GDG events, university and innovation hub hackathons (watch your department notice board), and online hackathons listed on Devpost and MLH.

## Prepare the week before

- **Build a mixed team:** a builder, a designer and a presenter. Not five backend developers.
- **Set up your tools:** Git, your framework and a starter template. Don't install on the day.
- **Learn one quick deploy** (Vercel, Render or similar), so your demo has a link.
- **Pack well:** charger, extension cable, water and a jacket for the AC.

Arrive rested. Tired teams make bad decisions.

## How the hours really go

| Hours | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Pick the problem and one core feature |
| 2–4 | Sketch screens, split tasks, set up Git |
| 4–30 | Build. Merge often. Keep it demo-able |
| 30–40 | Freeze features. Fix bugs, polish the demo |
| 40–46 | Slides and pitch practice, three times |
| 48 | Present. Breathe. Celebrate |

Golden rule: stop adding features at hour 30. A working demo beats a big idea.

## Choosing the idea

Pick a real problem you've seen (queues at the clinic, lost bus tickets, shop debts in a notebook), build one feature well, read the judging criteria, and make sure you can demo it in two minutes. Tanzanian problems make memorable projects.

## The 3-minute pitch

1. **The problem** (30 s): one real person, one real pain.
2. **The demo** (90 s): live, the main flow only.
3. **Why it matters** (30 s): who it helps and how many.
4. **What's next** (30 s): what you'd build with more time.

Record a demo video in case the Wi-Fi dies.

## Mistakes to avoid

Building too much · skipping all sleep (take naps in turns) · leaving the pitch to the last minute · arguing about the idea for hours (vote in 20 minutes and move on).

Losing your first hackathon is normal. Not going is the only fail.

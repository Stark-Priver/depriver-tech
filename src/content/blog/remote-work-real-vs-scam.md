“REMOTE JOB!! Earn $50/hour. No experience needed. Registration fee TZS 30,000. Contact HR on WhatsApp.” Every line of that is a warning. *Kazi halisi au utapeli?*

## Six red flags

1. **They ask you to pay:** registration, training or equipment. Real employers pay you.
2. **Huge pay, no skills needed.** If it sounds too good, it is.
3. **Only WhatsApp or Telegram:** no company website, no real email.
4. **Rush:** “apply TODAY”. Pressure stops you thinking.
5. **No interview:** hired after one chat message.
6. **They want your ID and bank details first**, before any contract.

Any job that asks you to pay first is a scam. Every time.

## Common scams

- **Registration fee:** pay to “secure” the job, then they vanish.
- **Task scams:** like videos or rate apps for small payouts, then “pay to unlock” bigger tasks. The early payouts are bait.
- **Fake equipment:** “buy your laptop from our supplier, we'll refund you.” They won't.
- **Data harvesting:** a fake form to collect your ID, NIDA number and bank details.

## What a real remote job looks like

A real company (website, LinkedIn page, real employees) → a clear role and pay range → interviews by video, maybe a small test → a written contract → they pay you. Real remote jobs need real skills, so build them first.

## Where to look

Company careers pages, LinkedIn jobs (check the company and the recruiter), known remote job boards such as We Work Remotely or Remote OK (still check each company), freelance platforms such as Upwork or Fiverr (where payment goes through the platform), and referrals from people you've worked with. Never move a freelance client off the platform before you've been paid.

## Verify before you send anything

Google the company name plus “scam”, check the email domain, find employees on LinkedIn, ask for a video call, and ask a mentor. Already paid? Stop, keep the evidence, report it and warn others.

## Getting paid

Agree on how, when and how much in writing, check that the payment method works in Tanzania **before** you sign, start small, and keep records (invoices, messages, references), for taxes too.
